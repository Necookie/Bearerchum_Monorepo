## Problem Statement
A crate of fruit is a grid of **R** rows and **C** columns. Each cell holds a fresh fruit (`F`) or a rotten fruit (`R`). (r, c) means row r from the top, column c from the left.

Every minute, each fresh fruit directly **above, below, left or right** of a rotten fruit turns rotten. Diagonal contact does not count. Print the number of minutes in which at least one fruit turned rotten.

All changes in a minute happen **at the same time**: a fruit that just turned rotten spreads only from the next minute on. Tip: simulate one minute at a time.

1. Mark every `F` that has an `R` above, below, left or right of it (for instance, change it to another letter such as `N`).
2. If nothing was marked, stop. Otherwise, change every marked cell to `R`, add 1 to the minutes, and go back to step 1.

For example, take the **3×3** crate shown under Start. Each minute it changes like this:

```
Start   Minute 1   Minute 2   Minute 3   Minute 4
FFF     FFF        RFF        RRF        RRR
FFF     RFF        RRF        RRR        RRR
RFF     RRF        RRR        RRR        RRR
```

The rot at (3, 1) reaches (2, 1) and (3, 2) in minute 1, then (1, 1), (2, 2) and (3, 3) in minute 2, (1, 2) and (2, 3) in minute 3, and (1, 3) in minute 4. The answer is **4** minutes.

## Input Format
- **R** and **C** on one line, separated by a space, after the prompt `Enter rows and columns: `
- The program then prints `Enter the crate:` on its own line and reads **R** lines of **C** characters each.

## Output Format
- `Minutes: M` on its own line, where **M** is the number of minutes in which at least one fruit turned rotten

## Constraints
- 1 ≤ R, C ≤ 10, and every character is `F` or `R`
- The crate may be a single row or a single column.
- There may be no rotten fruit; the answer is then 0.
- Rot from several rotten fruits spreads at the same time and may meet.
